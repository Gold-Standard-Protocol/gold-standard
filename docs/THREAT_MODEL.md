# Gold Standard Protocol: Threat Model

Structured by actor. For each actor we list the capability surface, the attacks available to them, and the mitigations (code-level, governance-level, or operational). Intended as the living registry of known risks.

See `SPECIFICATION.md` for formulas and invariants referenced below.

---

## Trust assumptions

The system is **only as safe as the weakest of these trust roots**:

1. **Ethereum L1 consensus** — assumed honest majority; standard.
2. **Chainlink PAXG/USD (the market feed C) and XAU/USD (the gold reference G)**: assumed to report within the staleness window and not be compromised. They are the only price sources, with no confirmation feed, pool source or keeper observation behind them (variant C). XAUT/USD joins only when XAUT is activated on its own push feed (`XAUT_ACTIVATION.md`); until then XAUT's oracle entry sits on an `InactiveFeed` that answers no price.
3. **Paxos + Tether custody of the underlying gold** — assumed solvent and not arbitrarily freezing our addresses.
4. **Governance multisig (proposer)**: assumed honest majority, 2-of-3 minimum. With the executors it also controls the timelock delay over time: the delay starts at 48 hours and can be changed once the change has waited out the delay in force (G10).
5. **Guardian multisig**: assumed honest in *raising* alarms (can pause, extend an active pause, freeze one token's oracle reads, which stops every seizure of that token during a pause as well, lift that freeze once a normal read would succeed and resync the oracle's `lastKnownPrice` record; cannot lift any pause or move funds: only governance unpauses early, and a vault, oracle, stability pool or reserve asset pause lapses after 14 days unless extended, as does a freeze).
6. **Keeper infrastructure** — assumed liveness, not integrity (liquidations just won't fire if keepers are down, but funds stay put).

Any single trust-root compromise should, at worst, pause the system rather than drain funds.

## Actor: External user (vault owner, swapper, GSUSD holder)

### Capabilities
- Open/close vaults, deposit/withdraw collateral within ratio limits, mint/repay GSUSD.
- Swap GSUSD for protocol-owned gold via `GoldStabilityReserve`.
- Interact with GSUSD as a standard ERC-20.

### Attacks
| # | Attack | Mitigation |
|---|---|---|
| U1 | Mint against under-valued collateral (price manipulation mid-tx) | Chainlink feeds are storage reads, not DEX spot. Borrowing prices at min(C, G) and only while C sits within the gold band (2%) of XAU/USD (`getBorrowPrice`), so a pushed market feed can never value collateral above gold. |
| U2 | Front-run own liquidation by topping up collateral | Expected — not an attack, just adversarial behaviour. Keepers must compete on gas. |
| U4 | Front-run a large swap to step the spread tier up against it (a swap lowers coverage, which can raise the spread for the next one) | Spread only ever rises as coverage falls, which favours the protocol; the constants bound it to `[0.5%, 5%]`; each swap pays the step of the coverage it leaves behind, so a large swap cannot drain across a step at the cheaper spread; `minGoldOut` lets the swapper abort if the quote moved between creation and inclusion; per-asset daily caps bound how far one actor can move coverage in a day. |
| U5 | Reentrancy via malicious ERC-20 in user-chosen field | Collateral tokens are governance-whitelisted (PAXG at launch; XAUT only after its activation batch). `nonReentrant` on every state-changing vault and reserve entry point. `SafeERC20` throughout. |
| U6 | Drain the reserve at a stale-low oracle: buy gold from the reserve below market while the feed lags a rising price | The spread is kept at or above the feed's deviation trigger (the MIN_SPREAD_PPM constant is 0.5%, equal to the PAXG/USD feed's 0.5% update trigger, so no governance curve can price a swap below what a lawfully lagging feed can move; resolved 2026-09-03, previously the constant allowed 0.1%); per-asset minimum reserve below which swaps refuse; per-asset daily cap on outflow (20% of the day-start balance at deploy) and an aggregate USD daily cap (off at deploy, governance-set); per-asset refusal on validation failure, because the swap prices off `getReservePrice`, max(C, G) only while both feeds are healthy and C sits inside the gold band, which reverts on a stale feed or a depeg specific to that token, so the swap is never priced off an unvalidated feed; guardian `pauseAsset(token)` per asset, lapsing after 14 days unless the guardian pauses again, early unpause governance-only. The reserve is the only party on the other side of this trade, so the residual is bounded by depth and caps, not by any vault. |
| U7 | The reserve counts gold it cannot pay out as coverage. Coverage is valued over every listed asset that is neither paused nor frozen, at `quoteValuationPrice`, which answers while the token is off its gold band; an asset whose daily cap is used up or set to zero, and gold an issuer has frozen or destroyed in the reserve's address (the ledger is not reconciled with the balance), are counted as well. With two assets listed, a swapper of the other asset then pays a narrower spread than the gold really available supports | Single asset at launch (PAXG), where the effect is on the spread step alone: a swap in the affected asset is refused anyway, and coverage that reads too high can only make the next swap cheaper, never pay out gold that is not there (the transfer fails). It becomes a cross-asset effect when XAUT is listed on the reserve. The control is the guardian's `pauseAsset(token)`, which removes the asset from coverage at once and belongs in the issuer-freeze and depeg runbooks; `XAUT_ACTIVATION.md` makes the two-asset case a criterion for listing XAUT on the reserve. |

U3 (redeem against an under-debted vault) was deleted on 2026-08-29 together with the vault-targeting redemption path (`GoldVault.redeemCollateral`, `RedemptionModule`); the number is retired. No peg operation by a third party can alter any borrower's collateral, debt or ratio, and the only function that reduces a vault's collateral without the owner's signature is liquidation. The Foundry `FeeConservation` suite asserts that no stability swap changed any vault.

## Actor: Liquidator / keeper

### Capabilities
- Call `liquidate()` and `liquidateViaPool()` against any underwater vault.
- Find those vaults on chain: `VaultRegistry.liquidatableVaults(offset, limit)` walks the list of open vaults the vault maintains, so no indexer or API stands between a liquidator and the protocol. The registry is a pure index; a wrong entry can only delay a liquidator, never move funds, and anyone can repair it with `sync`.
- Call `reserve.swapGsusdForGold()` to arbitrage the peg whenever GSUSD trades below $1 minus the spread.

### Attacks
| # | Attack | Mitigation |
|---|---|---|
| L1 | Manipulate oracle to push healthy vaults into liquidation range | Variant C (see O5): liquidation prices at min(max(C, G), G × (1 + m)), so a push of the market feed down values collateral at gold at worst and can liquidate only vaults inside PAXG's premium over gold; validated read and staleness window on both feeds. The retired 10% circuit breaker no longer exists. |
| L2 | Extract full penalty even on dust liquidations, cornering the market | `liquidatorIncentiveShare` is bounded (default 60%). On `liquidate` and `emergencyLiquidate` the protocol keeps the remaining share of the penalty; on `liquidateViaPool` the penalty goes to the keeper (half the liquidator share) and to Stability Pool depositors by design (docs/BUSINESS_MODEL.md), so a capitalised liquidator who is also the dominant depositor can take the whole penalty in one transaction, which is the pool's reward for standing ready. `minDebt` (governed within 100 to 10,000 GSUSD: 100 on Sepolia, 1,000 at the mainnet launch) prevents dust positions on every path, including partial liquidations, and a repayment that would seize no collateral is refused. |
| L3 | Use flash loan to liquidate, then return loan from seized collateral | Expected — liquidations *should* be capital-efficient. No mitigation needed; design is permissionless. |
| L5 | Just-in-time capture: deposit into the stability pool, liquidate through it and leave in one transaction, taking the gain standing depositors underwrite. There are two ways out of the pool: `withdraw` for the GSUSD and `claimCollateral` for the gold, and a liquidation that absorbs nearly the whole pool turns a deposit into gold, so the claim alone is an exit | Closed for the atomic form: `withdraw` (since 2026-10-22) and `claimCollateral` (since 2026-11-23) both refuse in the block of the caller's latest deposit (`No same-block withdrawal`, `No same-block claim`), and no other function pays GSUSD or gold out of the pool to a depositor; a gain belongs to the depositing address and cannot be assigned. Borrowed GSUSD therefore cannot be deposited, absorbed and taken out as gold inside one transaction, which without the claim rule took the whole penalty on any liquidation larger than the standing pool (an empty pool included) for no capital. The claim rule holds during a pool pause; claims are otherwise not paused. What the rule does not stop, by design: a liquidator who holds real GSUSD across a block boundary can deposit, liquidate through the pool and claim one block later, taking the penalty in proportion to its share of the pool (all of it when the pool was empty). That is the pool path as designed (L2): the depositor carries the price of the gold for at least a block and the protocol's share of the penalty is not collected on that path. A depositor who tops up waits one block for gains earned before the top-up; nothing is lost. |
| L6 | Vaults nobody liquidates: below 100% the penalty is zero, and the smallest vaults pay less than gas | Closed 2026-10-22: underwater gold sells at a governed discount (`underwaterDiscountPpm`, 2%, at most 5%) so the call pays and the bad debt is booked, capped at the shortfall so a vault a hair under water cannot hand the buffer's money to its own borrower. The discount is split like the penalty, 60% to the liquidator and 40% to the protocol, so a direct liquidator's margin on an underwater vault is at most 1.2% at launch; a pool liquidation pays its caller half of the liquidator share. The payment on such a liquidation retires principal before any accrued fee, so no stability fee is banked (or paid on to a fee beneficiary) from a vault whose principal was not recovered, and the bad debt booked is exactly the principal lost. Where the gold covers the principal but not the whole accrued fee, the unpaid part of the fee is written off when the vault closes and no `BadDebt` event is emitted, since no GSUSD is left unbacked; operators see it by comparing the amount a liquidation repaid with the vault's debt just before it (LAUNCH.md, pre-launch checklist, monitoring and alerting). Third-party liquidators therefore earn little on a vault that is barely under water, and the protocol's keeper is the reliable taker; `minDebt` is governed (100 on Sepolia, 1,000 at the mainnet launch) so the smallest vault's penalty share covers gas. |
| L7 | Issuer freezes the GoldVault address, which every liquidation routed collateral through | Closed 2026-10-22: liquidators are paid straight from the silos; the protocol's share is pulled to the vault for routing and, when that pull fails, goes to the liquidator too, so the seizure settles. Deposits still enter through the vault and would stop; that is not a solvency path. Since 2026-11-06 the vault first makes a zero-value transfer to itself, which never reverts, and cedes the share at once when the issuer refuses it, so a frozen vault settles at the same cost whatever the silo count (about 230,000 gas with 851 funded silos, against no settlement inside a 2^24 gas transaction from about 836 before). A caller who starves the probe reverts the whole liquidation, so the probe cannot be used to make an unfrozen vault cede. Listing condition: a collateral token must accept a zero-value transfer to the vault, or every liquidation would cede the protocol share (XAUT_ACTIVATION.md section 3). |
| L8 | A single underwater vault spread over about 1,000 silos cannot be liquidated: below 100% only a full liquidation is accepted, and it must fit in one 2^24 gas transaction | Recorded 2026-11-11, accepted for launch, no contract change. `test/LiquidationGas.test.ts` measures a direct liquidation at 522k, 589k, 669k and 830k gas for 1, 5, 10 and 20 silos, about 16k gas per extra silo; extrapolated to the 16,777,216 gas cap that is roughly 1,000 silos, about 100,000 oz in one vault at the 100 oz silo cap (roughly 400 million USD at 4,000 USD an ounce), and the vault must also be below 100%. The figure is an extrapolation from 20 silos, not a measurement at that size. Above 100% partial liquidations work at any size, so the bound bites only a vault of that size that is already under water; its bad debt could then not be booked until the price recovered or the owner repaid. Unreachable at launch volumes. Operators watch the largest vault's silo count against this bound (MAINNET.md section 5, the weekly review). Governance can raise the silo cap for later deposits (`setSiloCap`, at most 1,000 oz), which lowers the silo count of new vaults. |
| L9 | Pool depositors leave ahead of a loss-making absorption. While one gold token trades below gold the pool overpays for what it absorbs (residual 8). A depositor who sees the depeg, or the `liquidateViaPool` transaction itself, withdraws first: `withdraw` does not check for vaults waiting to be liquidated | Accepted; no contract change. The protocol loses nothing: a loss the documents already disclose falls on the depositors who stay. The same-block rule (L5) does not apply to a depositor of long standing. The guardian can pause the pool, which stops withdrawals, but absorptions continue through a pause, so a pause holds depositors in rather than protecting them and is not used for this. |
| L4 | MEV: sandwich stability swaps or liquidations | Known MEV exposure. Operator-run keepers to use private mempools. Slippage-protected swaps (`minGoldOut`) limit damage from spread-tier griefing. |

## Actor: Governance (proposer multisig + timelock + executor)

### Capabilities
- Propose parameter changes; each executes only after the timelock delay in force, 48 hours at deployment.
- Change that delay itself through `TimelockController.updateDelay`, once the change has waited out the delay in force (G10).
- Swap oracle feeds, stability reserve, surplus buffer, stability pool.
- Configure the stability reserve: assets, minimum reserve, daily caps, spread curve within the constant bounds, aggregate daily cap, penalty share; withdraw reserve gold or GSUSD inventory; unpause an asset.
- Update ratios, penalties, ceilings, fees.
- Withdraw accumulated surplus.

### Attacks
| # | Attack | Mitigation |
|---|---|---|
| G1 | Governance compromise: proposer keys stolen, propose malicious parameter | The timelock delay (48 hours at deployment) gives users time to repay and withdraw; a change to the delay itself must first wait out the delay in force (G10). Community monitoring + alerting on every scheduled proposal, read from the timelock's own `CallScheduled` events, because a proposer can schedule on the timelock directly and the wrapper's `ProposalCreated` then never fires. |
| G8 | Governance (or its stolen keys) grants `MINTER_ROLE` to a new address after the timelock delay: unlimited mint | `GoldStablecoin.lockMinters(vault)` (2026-09-26): one-way, admin-only, removes the power to grant `MINTER_ROLE` from every key including the timelock. Taken at steady state (LAUNCH.md step 5) and enforced by `verify-deployment.ts` with `EXPECT_MINTERS_LOCKED=1`. Since 2026-11-06 the stablecoin is `AccessControlEnumerable` and the lock succeeds only while `vault` is the sole `MINTER_ROLE` holder and the caller the sole `DEFAULT_ADMIN_ROLE` holder, so a minter or admin planted before the handover can never be frozen inside it. |
| G9 | Governance grants `VAULT_ROLE` on the `SurplusBuffer` to an address it controls and calls `absorbBadDebt(balance)` to take the reserve past the automatic 2% floor | Closed 2026-10-29: `absorbBadDebt` burns the absorbed GSUSD inside the buffer and pays the caller nothing, so a role holder can at most destroy buffer GSUSD, which makes the remaining supply better backed, and never take it. `GoldVault` books the absorption from the fall in GSUSD supply, capped at the outstanding bad debt, so a misbehaving buffer can neither revert a liquidation nor underflow `totalBadDebt`. Surplus leaves the buffer only through `withdrawGsusd`, which keeps the floor. |
| G11 | An approval outlives the moment it was made for: an operation that matured but was never executed (an `unpause` that reverted because the pause lapsed first, a superseded parameter proposal) is executed later, by anyone under an open executor, to lift a later guardian pause or reverse a later decision | Bounded since 2026-11-06. `GoldGovernance` deploys an `ExpiringTimelockController`: an operation can be executed only within `GRACE_PERIOD` (7 days) of becoming ready and then reverts `TimelockOperationExpired` for good, so a decision that missed its window is scheduled again and waits out the delay again. The ratios and the ceiling have separate setters (G2). The executor is a restricted Safe unless `ALLOW_OPEN_EXECUTOR=1` accepts the zero address (G5), and `verify-deployment.ts` asserts the grace period (`timelock:grace-period-7d`). An unpause also names the pause it was approved for: `unpause(epoch)` on the vault, oracle and stability pool takes `pauseEpoch()`, and `unpauseAsset(token, epoch)` on the reserve takes `assetPauseEpoch(token)`. Every pause, extension, renewal and bad-debt breaker trip takes a new number, so an approval given before the hold was last re-asserted reverts `Different pause`. A governance `guardianUnfreezeToken(token, epoch)` names the freeze the same way (`freezeEpoch(token)`, which every `freezeToken` call advances), so an unfreeze that matured after its freeze lapsed reverts `Different freeze` against a later freeze instead of lifting it for a liquidation in the same transaction. The cost of the numbering: a compromised guardian can void an unpause scheduled before its revocation executes (GD1), and while bad debt is over the threshold any bad-debt liquidation voids a pending vault unpause, so the change that makes an unpause safe is always its own operation first (LAUNCH.md, possible exploit in flight). Rewiring operations (`setOracle`, `setFeed`, `setAnchorFeed`) are not numbered: a matured rewiring that is no longer wanted is cancelled before it can sidestep a later freeze or pause. Residual: inside the 7 days the executor still chooses the moment and a parameter setter applies its value whatever has happened since scheduling, so the proposer Safe lists the pending operations weekly and after every incident and cancels any that is superseded, reverted or whose pause has lapsed (LAUNCH.md, the timelock queue). |
| G10 | Governance (or its stolen proposer keys, together with executor keys unless execution is open) shortens or removes the timelock delay by scheduling `TimelockController.updateDelay` directly on the timelock, outside the wrapper's `propose*` helpers | Disclosed authority, not a bypass. `GoldGovernance` deploys an `ExpiringTimelockController` (OpenZeppelin's `TimelockController` with a 7 day grace period on matured operations, G11) with a 48 hour minimum delay (`MIN_DELAY`) and its `propose*` helpers always schedule with that delay, but the configured proposers and executors also hold the timelock's own proposer and executor roles, so a proposer can schedule any call on the timelock, `updateDelay` included. The minimum delay starts at 48 hours; every change, including a change to the delay itself, must first wait out the delay in force, in public on chain (`CallScheduled`), and its execution emits `MinDelayChange`. Once executed, the new delay, even zero, applies to every later operation: a reduction to zero can be followed at once by a zero delay operation. Proposers and executors therefore control the delay policy over time. There is no bypass of the initial waiting period and no privilege escalation. Monitoring watches the timelock's own events, not only the wrapper's `ProposalCreated`, and a re-run of `verify-deployment.ts` fails `timelock:minDelay-48h` once the delay differs from 48 hours. An immutable minimum would need a different contract and a separate change. |
| G12 | Governance (or its stolen proposer and executor keys) grants `VAULT_ROLE` on the `StabilityPool` to an address it controls, which calls the pool's absorb function with no collateral sent and receives up to all but one wei of the depositors' GSUSD | Recorded 2026-11-11, accepted under the governance trust assumption. The pool has one absorb function, `absorbLiquidationPrefunded(token, debtToAbsorb)`: it pays the GSUSD asked for and credits depositors with whatever collateral arrived since the last absorption, nothing included, so it trusts its caller entirely. An older pull-style `absorbLiquidation(token, debtToAbsorb, collateralAmount)`, which the vault never called, has been removed, so that entry point no longer exists; the trust in the role holder is unchanged, because the remaining function gives a holder the same GSUSD (`test-foundry/residuals/PoolVaultRoleHolder.t.sol`). The pool's `VAULT_ROLE` has no lock, unlike the silo factory's (G7) and the minter set (G8); its admin is `DEFAULT_ADMIN_ROLE`, the timelock after the handover, so the grant needs a timelock operation and waits 48 hours. The defence is the queue: alert on every `CallScheduled`, with a separate rule for one whose target is the stability pool and whose call is `grantRole`, and have the proposer Safe cancel any grant governance did not intend (LAUNCH.md, the timelock queue and the pre-launch checklist). Depositors can withdraw during the delay unless the pool is paused. |
| G13 | A proposer or executor Safe is compromised and governance removes it from the timelock's roles only. The `GoldGovernance` wrapper keeps a second `PROPOSER_ROLE` and `EXECUTOR_ROLE` of its own and is itself a permanent proposer and executor on the timelock, so the removed Safe still schedules through every `propose*` helper and still executes through `executeProposal` | Two role lists for one authority, and nothing on chain keeps them in step. Bounded by the delay in force and by the cancellers, who see every `CallScheduled`. The rule is operational: a rotation revokes and grants on the timelock and on the wrapper in one proposal, with the calls in `LAUNCH.md` (Governance signer key compromised). `verify-deployment.ts` fails (`gov:wrapper-roles-match-timelock`) when the wrapper's holders are not exactly the timelock's proposers and executors other than the wrapper, naming the stale holder and the side it remains on. |
| G14 | Governance (or its stolen proposer and executor keys) grants `VAULT_ROLE` on the `StabilityModule`, the `SurplusBuffer` or the `GoldStabilityReserve` to an address it controls. On the module the holder calls `recordDebt` to give any vault a debt it never drew, which makes that vault liquidatable and sells its gold, or `clearDebt` to erase an accomplice's debt. On the buffer it calls `absorbBadDebt` and burns the buffer's GSUSD (G9: it can destroy, not take). On the reserve it can only deposit collateral | Accepted under the governance trust assumption, like G12, with no contract change. None of these roles is locked, unlike the silo factory's (G7) and the minter set (G8); each is administered by the timelock, so a grant needs a timelock operation and waits out the delay. The role census in `verify-deployment.ts` asserts each set is exactly the vault (plus the reserve on the buffer). The defence is the queue and the logs: alert on every `CallScheduled` whose call is `grantRole` on any of these contracts or on the pool, and on every `RoleGranted` for `VAULT_ROLE` on the module, the buffer, the reserve and the pool and for `MINTER_ROLE` on the stablecoin, and have the proposer Safe cancel what governance did not intend (`LAUNCH.md`, pre-launch checklist and the timelock queue). |
| G2 | Governance proposes to set `minRatio < liquidationRatio` (nonsense) | `updateCollateralRatios` (like `addCollateralType`) runs `_checkCollateralParams`, which asserts `minRatio <= MAX_MIN_RATIO` (1,000%), `minRatio > liquidationRatio`, `liquidationRatio >= 105%` (100% plus the largest liquidation cap the oracle accepts, 5%, so a market price pushed up to the cap cannot value a vault that is under water at gold back above its liquidation ratio) and `penalty <= 20%`; an update also requires an active collateral type. `GoldGovernance.proposeCollateralRatioUpdate` repeats these checks when it schedules the change, and the launch preflight applies the same limits to the environment (`LIMITS` in `scripts/lib/launchEnv.ts`). The debt ceiling has its own setter (`setDebtCeiling`) and its own proposal (`proposeDebtCeilingUpdate`), so a pending ratio proposal cannot restore an old ceiling and a pending ceiling proposal cannot restore old ratios. |
| G3 | Governance swaps oracle to a malicious one | Highest-risk parameter. It waits out the single timelock delay, 48 hours at deployment and itself governable (G10). A longer delay for an oracle replacement than for routine parameters is possible but is not built: it would need a second timelock. |
| G4 | Governance raises debt ceiling faster than collateral absorbs | Mainnet launches with no PAXG debt ceiling and no per-block mint cap (`LAUNCH_CAPS=open`), so supply is bounded by the collateral ratios alone (residual 4). Any ceiling governance sets later waits out the timelock delay (48 hours at deployment). Monitoring: Phase 4 solvency dashboard. |
| G5 | Proposer and executor roles collapse onto same multisig | Ceremony runbook explicitly separates them, and the preflight fails a Safe that holds two roles on mainnet. The executor is a Safe: `address(0)` (anyone may execute a matured operation) is refused on mainnet unless `ALLOW_OPEN_EXECUTOR=1` accepts it, because it hands everyone the choice of when a stale approval lands (G11; LAUNCH.md, executor policy). |
| G6 | Governance transfers admin to an EOA instead of timelock | Ceremony verification: `verify-deployment.ts` with `EXPECT_GOVERNANCE_TRANSFERRED=1` asserts timelock holds `DEFAULT_ADMIN_ROLE` on all nine governed contracts (stablecoin, oracle, vault, stability module, surplus buffer, stability pool, reserve, silo factory and the `GoldGovernance` wrapper) and the deployer on none; separately it asserts the timelock's minimum delay is 48 hours, its grace period 7 days (`timelock:grace-period-7d`) and that the deployer holds no timelock role. The role census (`census:*`, `scripts/lib/roleCensus.ts`) then requires every privileged role's whole holder set to be exactly the intended one, so a second admin beside the timelock fails as well (D1). |
| G7 | Holder of `DEFAULT_ADMIN_ROLE` on `SiloFactory` grants itself `VAULT_ROLE` and calls `withdraw()` on every silo while the vault ledger still shows the gold as deposited | **Closed 2026-08-29.** `SiloFactory.lockVaultRole(vault)` is called by `deploy.ts` right after the vault is granted the role: the admin of `VAULT_ROLE` becomes `LOCKED_ROLE`, so no key can ever grant or revoke `VAULT_ROLE` again. Since 2026-11-06 `LOCKED_ROLE` is enforced empty: it administers itself from the constructor, so nobody can ever be granted it, and `lockVaultRole` also requires it to have no member, so a pre-lock admin cannot keep a key that becomes the permanent admin of `VAULT_ROLE`. `verify-deployment.ts` asserts `custody:siloFactory.VAULT_ROLE-locked`, `custody:siloFactory.LOCKED_ROLE-self-administered` and that a `grantRole` from the signer reverts, and its role census reads both sets with `getRoleMember`: `LOCKED_ROLE` empty and `VAULT_ROLE` the vault alone. Collateral can then only leave a silo through the vault, and the only vault path that moves collateral without the owner's signature is liquidation below the threshold. Deployments made before the lock were exposed to their admin key; the current Sepolia deployment (2026-11-23) carries the lock. |

## Actor: Guardian multisig

### Capabilities
- Call `emergencyPause()` on `GoldOracle`, `GoldVault` and `StabilityPool` instantly (no timelock) and `pauseAsset(token)` on `GoldStabilityReserve` per asset; restart an active pause's 14 day window with `extendPause()`, or for a reserve asset by calling `pauseAsset` again. Each of these takes a new pause number, so it also voids any unpause governance scheduled before it (G11).
- Freeze one token's oracle reads (`freezeToken`, lapses after 14 days unless extended) and lift the freeze (`guardianUnfreezeToken(token, epoch)` with `epoch` from `freezeEpoch(token)`, which also works while paused) only when a normal liquidation read would succeed. Every `freezeToken` call takes a new freeze number. The freeze holds through a pause: `getEmergencyPrice` refuses a frozen token in every case, so a pause, the bad-debt breaker's included, never reopens `emergencyLiquidate` or `liquidateViaPool` on it.
- **Cannot** unpause (requires governance timelock).
- **Cannot** move funds, rewire modules, or change parameters.

### Attacks
| # | Attack | Mitigation |
|---|---|---|
| GD5 | A genuine incident outlasts the 14 day window: at expiry anyone could lift the pause and act in the same transaction before the guardian re-paused | Closed 2026-10-22: `extendPause` restarts the window while paused, with the pause authority; governance can still unpause early or revoke a guardian that abuses it. Since 2026-11-06 every extension, and every bad-debt breaker trip while paused, takes a new `pauseEpoch()`, so an unpause approved before it cannot lift the renewed hold (G11). |
| GD6 | The circuit breaker (permissionless, and the keeper sent it) paused the whole oracle for a genuine move; only governance could unpause, 48 h later | Closed: no permissionless breaker remains (a permissionless freeze is a stall vector). Each token's reads refuse on their own when its sources fail the rules, and resume when they pass; the guardian's `freezeToken` is a manual stop, lifted only when a normal read would succeed (2026-10-29). |
| GD7 | One token depegs from gold: the old global breaker froze every token and emergency liquidation priced at the stale pre-depeg price | Closed 2026-10-29: per-token rules, variant C of model section 15.8. Liquidation prices at min(max(C, G), G × (1 + m)), so a gold-wide crash liquidates at once and a depeg specific to the token is valued at gold, not at its market; borrowing and reserve swaps in that token stop while it sits off the gold band, and other tokens are untouched. Residual, accepted: a PAXG-specific depeg is never liquidated, and GSUSD holders carry the loss (4.5% of D at a 20% depeg, 16% at 30%, model 15.8). XAUT is inactive at launch (`XAUT_ACTIVATION.md`). |
| GD1 | Compromised guardian repeatedly pauses to grief the protocol | Unpause requires timelock: governance can revoke guardian role via standard proposal (`revokeRole(GUARDIAN_ROLE, compromised)`). Since 2026-09-26 a pause also lapses after 14 days (`unpauseExpired`, permissionless) unless the guardian restarts the window with `extendPause` (GD5), and since 2026-11-03 so does a reserve asset pause, so a compromised guardian can hold a pause until the revocation executes. Since 2026-11-06 every extension also voids an unpause scheduled before it (G11), so governance schedules `revokeRole` on its own and the unpause once it has executed: a batch that revokes and unpauses together is voided whole by one extension, revocation included, and the hold then lasts at most one more timelock delay past the revocation. `repay` stays open and a debt-free vault can withdraw during any pause, so griefing can delay but never lock funds. |
| GD4 | Governance keys lost or governance absent after a pause (guardian, breaker or bad-debt) | Closed 2026-09-26: pauses expire after `MAX_PAUSE_DURATION` and `withdraw` works for debt-free vaults while paused, so users can always repay in full and exit with no key involved. The reserve's per-asset pause had no such lapse; it stopped only conversion, never a vault exit, and since 2026-11-03 it lapses on its own after `ASSET_PAUSE_DURATION` (14 days) unless the guardian pauses again, as the oracle's token freeze does after `FREEZE_DURATION`. A freeze that governance cannot lift because its keys are lost therefore stops a token's seizures for at most 14 days past the guardian's last renewal. |
| GD2 | Guardian pauses the vault while gold crashes: `emergencyLiquidate` stays live but borrowers cannot defend | Closed 2026-09-05: `deposit` works while paused (it needs no oracle and strictly improves the position), so a borrower can always top up against an emergency liquidation. `repay` is not pause-gated either, and `withdraw` refuses during a pause only while the vault still carries debt (`Paused: repay in full to withdraw`), so repaying in full and withdrawing stays open (GD4). The vault pause gates `mint` and `liquidate` (`whenNotPaused`) and an indebted `withdraw`; `liquidateViaPool` keeps running at the emergency price, and `emergencyLiquidate` runs only during a pause; neither seizes a token the guardian has frozen. |
| GD8 | Guardian fails to pause during a real incident (liveness) | Guardian is defence-in-depth. The oracle needs no pause to stay safe: each token's priced paths refuse on their own while a feed is unhealthy and resume when it answers again. |
| GD3 | Guardian doxxed, attacker targets signer personally | Operational: geographically distributed signers, hardware keys, alternate identities for signers where feasible. |

## Actor: Oracle (Chainlink aggregators)

### Capabilities
- Feed price data consumed by vault and stability reserve.

### Attacks
| # | Attack | Mitigation |
|---|---|---|
| O1 | Chainlink feed stalls (no update for hours) | Validated read with `stalenessThreshold`: while C or G is unhealthy every priced path for the token is refused (`No reliable price`; `No price` on the emergency path), emergency liquidation included, and resumes by itself on the first healthy pair. `lastKnownPrice` never prices a seizure and the resyncs only record what the rules give. Residual (model 15.8 point 2): a C outage during a 30% one-hour gold fall leaves about 17.7% of D to the buffer, then GSUSD holders; a G outage during a crash refuses liquidation the same way. |
| O2 | Chainlink feed returns zero/negative/reverts | The validated read treats a non-positive answer, an incomplete round, a future stamp or a revert as unhealthy and refuses that token's priced paths; nothing pauses and nothing needs resetting. |
| O3 | Chainlink deprecates the feed and stops updating | Operator: monitor Chainlink deprecation notices. Governance proposal to swap feed. Pre-drafted proposal kept ready. |
| O4 | Oracle manipulated by attacker with Chainlink node access | Out of threat model scope for L1 deployment: Chainlink reputation + economic security. No further source is planned: the project's rule set is two Chainlink sources per token and nothing else, with Pyth outside the production path (`ORACLE_IMPLEMENTATION_PLAN.md`). Variant C bounds what a corrupted market feed C can do (O5); XAU/USD has no second gold reference behind it. |
| O5 | Flash-price spike or manipulated market feed | Variant C (model 15.8): the market feed C is weighed only against XAU/USD (G). Borrowing needs C within 2% of G and prices at min(C, G), so it never prices above gold. Liquidation prices at min(max(C, G), G × (1 + m)), m = 2%: a push of C down values collateral at gold at worst, so it can only manufacture a liquidation inside PAXG's premium over gold (0.15% of D at the measured 0.71% premium), and a push up holds off a liquidation by at most m (+1.28% against the market measured). Reserve swaps need C inside the band and price at max(C, G). No pool, no breach record and no observation duty remain. PAXG launches with no debt ceiling (`LAUNCH_CAPS=open`, the only mainnet profile). Residual: the pushes above, borne by borrowers near 110% and, for a stall, by the buffer. |

## Actor: Collateral token issuer (Paxos, Tether)

### Capabilities
- Freeze balances of specific addresses.
- Pause their own token.
- Burn issuer-held tokens.
- Redeem underlying physical gold.

### Attacks
| # | Attack | Mitigation |
|---|---|---|
| C1 | Issuer freezes or destroys the gold in one silo address | Accepted issuer risk; what the contracts do is limited. `SiloFactory` holds collateral in many silo addresses, so one frozen address does not strand all the gold, and withdrawals and liquidations route around a silo that refuses to send. The loss is **not shared**. Collateral is fungible across silos, no vault's ledger and no `totalCollateral` figure is written down when a silo is frozen or its balance destroyed, and the unfrozen gold goes to whoever withdraws first. The vaults that leave last bear the whole loss: once the unfrozen gold is less than what a vault is owed, its withdrawal reverts `Withdrawal failed: insufficient unfrozen silo balance`. A public freeze is therefore a reason for every borrower in that token to repay and withdraw early. A freeze of the vault's own address is L7. |
| C2 | Issuer freezes all known silo addresses | Withdrawals and liquidations route around frozen silos (latest unfrozen silo first), and governance can skip deposits past a frozen run with `SiloFactory.setCurrentSiloIndex` (at most 64 per call). The index it sets is a floor (`minSiloIndex`): the cursor rewind after a withdrawal never goes back below it, so emptying the silo above a run of issuer-frozen empty silos cannot undo the skip. A deposit creates every silo missing up to the cursor, about 110,000 gas each, so after a long skipped run (three or more full moves) one deposit would need more than the 2^24 gas a transaction may carry, and on a network that prices new state at several times that a single 64-silo move already would; the cursor cannot be moved back. `SiloFactory.createSilos(token, count)` therefore creates the missing silos ahead of the deposit, at most 16 a call (about 1.8 million gas, under 55% of a transaction at five times the price). It is open to anyone because it creates exactly the silos the next deposit would create, at the same addresses, never one past the cursor, and moves no funds; it is refused (`Unknown token`) for an address that has no silo and whose cursor governance never moved, so nobody can create a silo or a `SiloCreated` event for an address that is not a collateral token. Operators run it after every move until `missingSilos(token)` is 0, as separate transactions and never inside an atomic batch, because it reverts once the gap is filled (LAUNCH.md). Every skipped silo stays in the list a withdrawal walks when it has to reach gold below the run (about 10,000 gas each), which is the cost of the skip itself and one reason each move is bounded and reviewed. Rotation is **not** available while gold is frozen: `GoldVault.setSiloFactory` refuses to replace or unwire a factory until `isEmpty()` is true, and a frozen silo never drains, because `GoldVault` reads one factory at a time and would strand the gold. Rotation therefore needs the issuer to release the frozen gold, or a new governed legacy-withdrawal path that keeps the old factory as a withdrawal source, which is not built. Nothing turns frozen gold into bad debt. A liquidation, by any of the three paths, pays its seizure out of the silos, so one that cannot reach enough unfrozen gold reverts as a whole: the vault keeps its debt, no `BadDebt` event is emitted, `totalBadDebt` does not move and the `SurplusBuffer` absorbs nothing. The GSUSD that vault minted stays outstanding against gold nobody can seize until the issuer releases the gold or the borrower repays; if the balance was destroyed, the factory never reads as empty again and so can never be replaced. The consequences are an unrecorded shortfall that monitoring must find by comparing `totalCollateral` with the silos' balances, and liquidations in that token that stop working once the unfrozen gold runs short. `setSiloFactory` also refuses to wire a factory over collateral the vault already holds itself. |
| C3 | Issuer becomes insolvent (physical gold custody fails) | Systemic counterparty risk. No on-chain mitigation. Diversification across PAXG + XAUT would reduce single-custodian exposure, but PAXG is the only active collateral at launch, so Paxos is a single custodian until XAUT is activated. User-facing disclosure required. |
| C4 | Issuer pauses token transfers entirely | Nothing in the protocol pauses on its own: every path that moves the token (deposits, withdrawals, the collateral leg of every liquidation and reserve swaps in it) fails while the issuer's pause lasts, and repayment, which moves only GSUSD, stays open. Vaults in the token cannot be liquidated until transfers resume, so a price fall meanwhile can leave bad debt; governance reacts via migration or unwind proposal. |
| C5 | Issuer updates token logic to implement transfer fees | PAXG has no transfer fee today: Paxos removed the mechanism in its June 2026 upgrade. The token is an upgradeable proxy, so a fee can return. **Partial coverage**: deposit flow uses before/after balance measurement (fee-on-transfer safe). Withdrawal sends fixed amount; user receives less than expected but does not break invariants. If the fee is ever switched on it is charged on every hop, and the protocol makes more hops than a plain transfer: a deposit moves the token from the user to the vault and from the vault to a silo (two fees), and the protocol's share of a penalty moves from a silo to the vault and on to the reserve or the buffer (two fees, three once a FeeSplitter stands in front of the buffer). Every credit is a measured arrival, so the ledgers stay right; the cost falls on depositors and on the protocol's share. A fee charged on top of the amount sent, rather than taken from it, would drain silos faster than the ledgers fall and is the case to test on a fork after any upgrade of the token. |

## Actor: Deployer (pre-transfer)

### Capabilities (temporary — before `transferGovernance`)
- Holds `DEFAULT_ADMIN_ROLE` and `GOVERNANCE_ROLE` on every contract.
- Can grant/revoke any role.
- Can change any parameter without timelock.

### Attacks
| # | Attack | Mitigation |
|---|---|---|
| D1 | Deployer is malicious or compromised during the ceremony window | Ceremony runbook: fresh deployer EOA, hardware wallet, single-use private key, `transferGovernance` called in the same session. No user funds held during this window (minting opens only with the governance handover). `transferGovernance` revokes the caller alone, so a role the deployer grants another key, or a non-admin role it grants itself (the wrapper's proposer and executor roles), would survive the handover; since 2026-11-06 the role census catches it. `transfer-governance.ts` takes the census before its first transaction and again after the handover, and `verify-deployment.ts` takes it on every run: each privileged role's holder set, rebuilt from `getRoleMember` and the role logs, must be exactly the intended one, a role no contract defines must have no holder and the deployer must hold no role the configuration does not name it for. `lockMinters(vault)` refuses a minter set that is not exactly the vault (G8). |
| D2 | Deployer forgets to revoke own roles before handing off | `verify-deployment.ts` with `EXPECT_GOVERNANCE_TRANSFERRED=1` catches this, through the `gov:*` checks and the role census (`census:deployer`). Runbook explicitly requires running verification before opening the system. |
| D3 | Deployer sets wrong guardian/proposer/executor addresses | Runbook: two-engineer review of env vars; the role census asserts every guardian, proposer and executor set is exactly the snapshot's; Sepolia dry-run first. |
| D4 | The contract that creates the token keeps a role on it, or someone takes the token's address first | `GoldStablecoinDeployer` creates GSUSD with CREATE2 at an address fixed in advance. The token gives its admin role to its creator, so that contract passes the role to the deployer and renounces its own in the same transaction, and only its owner, the deployer, can call it. `deploy.ts` asks it for the address before the token is created and stops if the address is not `GSUSD_ADDRESS`, then checks that the deployer is the admin and the contract is not. `verify-deployment.ts` asserts the same from the chain (`token:*`), and the role census counts the grant and the renunciation. The contract creates one token and can do nothing afterwards. |

## Residual risks (accepted / not mitigated)

1. **Regulatory seizure** — off-chain legal action could force freezing, delisting, or user blocking. Operator maintains counsel on standby.
2. **MEV on small swaps.** Users swapping small amounts through the reserve will see worse effective prices due to gas + MEV. Acceptable; the swap is intended primarily for arbitrage at peg deviations.
3. **Smart contract risk.** Tests, fuzzing and invariants cover critical properties but not full functional correctness. Bugs may remain despite testing, and a bug could cause a loss of funds.
4. **Tail-event depeg** — a >50% gold price crash over minutes could cause cascading liquidations. Over-collateralisation (130% to mint or withdraw, liquidation below 110%) absorbs typical volatility. Mainnet launches with no debt ceiling (`LAUNCH_CAPS=open`), so exposure during stress is bounded by the collateral ratios, the bad-debt auto-pause and the liquidation paths rather than by a ceiling.
5. **Floor-absent state when the reserve is empty.** The reserve ships empty and refuses every swap (`Reserve insufficient`) until it holds gold above the minimum reserve; once its redeemable gold or daily cap is exhausted the guaranteed conversion stops until it refills from liquidation penalties or seeding. During that state GSUSD has no floor other than a borrower's incentive to buy back their own debt. The state is visible on-chain (`redeemableCollateral`, `coveragePpm`, `remainingDailyCapacity`), `verify-deployment` warns on it, and it is stated as a limitation in every document that claims the floor.
6. **Pressure to let the reserve issue GSUSD.** A persistent premium makes 1:1 issuance from the reserve look like a cheap fix. It is not a parameter change: it would move gold/USD risk on that GSUSD from borrowers onto the protocol with no loss absorber, since healthy borrower collateral cannot be appropriated to cover a reserve shortfall. It is structurally absent (the reserve holds no `MINTER_ROLE`, `verify-deployment` asserts it and the Foundry suite proves it), and enabling it would require its own junior capital, and a governance architecture change.
7. **Two-block spread grief (accepted risk, 1 November 2026).** A borrower mints a very large amount in block N and repays it in block N+1. The repayment's floor is the supply at the start of block N+1, which already includes the mint, so the smoothed supply records the inflated figure and the reserve prices its spread against it for about a day (from 0.5% to the restricted 2% at 20% coverage; still 1.25% twelve hours later). The single-block form stays closed. Nothing can be taken: the extra spread is paid to the protocol, not to the attacker, who pays gas, the stability fee for one block and the cost of holding the collateral (about $16M of PAXG for a 10M mint at 20% coverage); a flash loan cannot span two blocks. Swappers set `minGoldOut`, so a swap priced at the dearer tier is refused rather than filled unexpectedly. Evidence: `contracts/test-foundry/residuals/TwoBlockSpreadGrief.t.sol`. Mitigation armed at launch: `originationFeeBps` ships at 5 bps, so the mint leg now costs 0.05% of its size (5,000 GSUSD for a 10M mint), banked in the SurplusBuffer. Held in reserve: flooring the burn at the lowest supply over the last hour.
8. **Pool depositors carry a depeg in one gold token (disclosed, 1 November 2026).** Liquidation values collateral at gold at worst (GD7), so while PAXG alone trades below gold a vault under 110% at the gold price is still liquidated at the gold price. Through `liquidateViaPool` the pool pays the debt and receives PAXG worth less at market: at a 10% depeg about 7% of the absorbed debt, while the caller still earns the keeper share. Depositors choose this exposure by depositing, and the agent guide and the liquidations docs say so. Evidence: `contracts/test-foundry/residuals/DepegPoolOverpays.t.sol`.

## Transaction ordering risks, in one place

Every path where the order of transactions decides who gains, with where it is treated. None lets an outsider take collateral or mint; each moves a bounded amount between users.

| Entry point | What ordering gives | Who gains, and the bound | Where |
|---|---|---|---|
| `GoldStabilityReserve.swapGsusdForGold` | A swap placed ahead of another lowers coverage and can move the later swap to a wider spread step; a sandwich around a small swap takes part of its value | The front-runner, up to the difference between two steps; the victim's `minGoldOut` refuses a worse fill. The swap has no deadline, so a delayed transaction fills at the price of its own block within `minGoldOut` | U4, L4, residual 2 |
| `GoldStabilityReserve.swapGsusdForGold` | Buying reserve gold while the feed lags a rising price | The swapper, by less than the spread, which is never below the feed's update trigger; the daily cap bounds the size, and a fixed UTC day lets the cap be used on both sides of midnight | U6, SPECIFICATION §3.8 |
| `GoldVault.liquidate`, `liquidateViaPool` | Liquidations are a race; the later transaction reverts `Not undercollateralised` | The faster liquidator. The borrower loses nothing extra: the price is the oracle's, not the pool's or the caller's | L4 |
| `StabilityPool.deposit` then `liquidateViaPool` | Depositing just before an absorption to share its gain | A depositor with its own GSUSD held across a block boundary, in proportion to its share; the atomic form with borrowed GSUSD is closed | L5 |
| `StabilityPool.withdraw` | Leaving just before a loss-making absorption | The depositor who leaves, at the expense of those who stay | L9, residual 8 |
| `GoldVault.mint` then `repay` in the next block | Raising the smoothed supply for a day, which widens the spread and raises the buffer's floor | Nobody: the wider spread is paid to the protocol and the griefer pays the origination fee | Residual 7 |
| `GoldStablecoin.burn` | Any burn, of any size, re-banks the smoothed supply and slows its decay | Nobody: it can only keep the spread wider for longer | SPECIFICATION §3.8 |
| Timelock `execute`, `GoldGovernance.executeProposal` | The executor chooses the moment within the 7 day grace period | The executor Safe only; pauses, freezes and asset pauses are numbered so an old approval cannot lift a newer hold | G11 |
| `GoldOracle.guardianUnfreezeToken` against a scheduled unfreeze | A fresh freeze voids an unfreeze scheduled before it | The guardian, by at most one more delay | Trust assumption 5, SPECIFICATION §3.9 |

## Response plan (summarised)

| Trigger | Response |
|---|---|
| Oracle refuses a price (`No reliable price`) or C leaves the gold band | Keeper alert → on-call investigates the Chainlink feeds → nothing to reset when a feed recovers; a deprecated feed is replaced through a timelocked `setFeed` or `setAnchorFeed`. |
| Guardian pauses | Incident commander identified → public status update within 1 hour → post-mortem within 48h. |
| Reserve outflow abnormal or a feed suspected stale | Guardian `pauseAsset(token)` on the affected asset only (any other listed asset keeps swapping; PAXG is the only reserve asset at launch) → confirm the feed → governance reviews caps and minimum reserve and unpauses through the timelock with `unpauseAsset(token, assetPauseEpoch(token))`; the guardian pauses again within 14 days if the concern stands, or the pause lapses. |
| Known bug exploited | Pause via guardian → patched contract deployed → migration proposal through timelock (after `lockMinters`, a replacement vault needs a new GSUSD token) → user comms. |
| Issuer freezes silo | Estimate affected collateral → governance moves new deposits past the frozen silos (`SiloFactory.setCurrentSiloIndex`, C2) → user comms → any bad debt a liquidation books is absorbed from the `SurplusBuffer`, which only the `VAULT_ROLE` holder (the vault) can call (anyone can re-attempt with `GoldVault.retryBadDebtAbsorption`); replacing the silo factory waits until the issuer releases the frozen gold (C2). |
